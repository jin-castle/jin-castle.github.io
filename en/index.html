02 — Adjoint grating coupler

Inverse Design

Each pixel of the 70 nm etch layer is a density ρ (0 = etched, 1 = silicon). An intermediate ρ is a permittivity ε(ρ) between the two materials, so it is differentiable. Starting from uniform gray (ρ = 0.5), the optimization found a ~630 nm period and apodization on its own.

  1. ForwardCompute the electric field E in the design region from the incident light
  2. AdjointSend the target mode backward from the output waveguide to compute E†
  3. Gradient∂F/∂ε ∝ Re(E·E†) — the gradient for every pixel from two simulations
  4. UpdateMove ρ along the gradient, and raise β to push gray to 0/1
  • 45.3%2D after boundary sharpening, 1550 nm (res 160)
  • 44.5%3D FDTDX validation (−3.51 dB, estimate)
  • 0.4–2.3%adjoint gradient vs finite-difference error

05 — End-to-end AR optics

AR Waveguide

An exploded view of the optical module for one eye of AR glasses. Light from a micro display travels through a glass waveguide by total internal reflection and exits in front of the eye. I inverse-designed the in/out coupler gratings in this path and simulated how the image forms on the retina.

  • Λ 2µmFreeform in/out couplers + first-order fold
  • 17 × 3out-coupler optimization angles (fewer vertical stripes)
  • n 2.0Glass waveguide, 540 nm

I found that optimizing the unit cell at only a few angles lets Rayleigh anomalies between those angles draw vertical stripes across the image, and reduced them substantially by sampling more angles.
